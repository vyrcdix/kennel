# Steep for Life — A User's Guide for Busy Brains

*A companion to the main user's guide (`kennel-user-guide.md`). Same
product, same lifecycle, different life. This edition is for someone
whose interests don't fit in a work tool: you read constantly, you're
training for a marathon, you're planning a trip, you have a creative
project half-built in the garage, and you generate more ideas before
breakfast than you can act on in a month. If your brain is great at
*having* thoughts and less great at *keeping* them — this is the
manual.*

---

## 1. Why this works when your brain has forty tabs open

Steep is built on a few mechanics that happen to be exactly what a
distractible, many-interested brain needs:

**One inbox for everything.** Every stray thought goes to the same
place — **the bench** — regardless of which life it belongs to. You
never have to decide *where something goes* at the moment you think of
it. Deciding is a separate activity, done later, in bulk, with single
keystrokes. This is the load-bearing wall: capture and organize are
**two different moods**, and Steep never makes you do both at once.

**Forgiveness is built in.** Most systems punish you for ignoring
them: badges climb, tasks go red, the backlog becomes a guilt
monument. Steep has *no notifications, no red, no overdue*. Things you
ignore simply go **cold**, and there's a dedicated screen for sweeping
cold things with one keystroke each — most of them into the bin,
shamelessly. Abandoning an idea is a supported workflow, not a
failure state.

**"Not now" is a real answer.** The **Reflecting** shelf holds things
you've deliberately set aside. They aren't tasks. They aren't overdue.
They're just *shelved*, and there's a screen for walking the shelf
when you're in the mood.

**It remembers your own conclusions back at you.** The thing you
actually want from all this capturing isn't a tidy database — it's
that the lesson you learned in March is still *operational* in
August. Crystals (§7) resurface on a cadence and ask one question:
*still true?* It's spaced repetition for your own life.

**It's quiet.** Steep never pings you. You visit it; it doesn't chase
you. For a brain that's already over-interrupted, this is a feature
you'll feel in the first week.

## 2. Set up your threads — one per ongoing interest

A **thread** is a container for one ongoing line of your life. Don't
overthink the taxonomy — threads are cheap (`⌘⇧N`), renameable, and
closeable. A realistic starter set:

| Thread | Slug | What lives there |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | `reading` | What to read, what you're reading, what books did to you |
| Marathon 2027 | `marathon-2027` | Training, gear, race plans, what your body taught you |
| Japan trip | `japan-2027` | Research, bookings, itinerary, then the memories |
| The novel | `novel` | Scenes, character notes, structure ideas, drafts |
| Idea garden | `ideas` | Ideas that don't belong to any project *yet* |
| Household | `household` | The dishwasher noise, the gutter, the insurance renewal |

Two tips from the trenches:

- **Make an `ideas` thread.** Your brain produces ideas that have no
  home; without a designated garden they'll either clog another
  thread or you'll hesitate at capture ("where does this GO?") and
  hesitation kills capture. Sort them into the garden; promote them
  to their own thread if they ever sprout.
- **Pin only what's alive.** Pinned threads show in the nav rail and
  dashboard. Training and the trip are alive in March; the novel
  might not be. Unpinning isn't quitting — the thread keeps
  everything and waits.

## 3. Capture — the only habit you actually have to build

Everything else in Steep is optional. This isn't: **when a thought
arrives, put it on the bench and go back to your life.** Under five
seconds, no required fields, no decisions.

Click **Capture** (top bar), type, enter. Don't pick the perfect kind
— guess, or leave the default; you can convert later with one key.
Don't pick the thread — that's Sort's job, not capture's.

What a real bench looks like by Thursday:

> - *idea* — `audiobooks for long runs — does fiction work above 90 min?`
> - *note* — `that Le Guin line about how the end justifies the means only if the end is real`
> - *action* — `renew passport BEFORE booking Kyoto ryokan`
> - *question* — `is my left-knee thing a shoe problem or a mileage problem?`
> - *idea* — `novel: what if ch. 3 is from the sister's POV`
> - *action* — `call about the dishwasher noise`
> - *ref* — `that marathon-pacing calculator site`
> - *idea* — `birthday: mara mentioned wanting the seed-saving book`

Eight thoughts, five lives, zero filing decisions made. All safely
out of your head.

**The paste firehose.** For anything bigger than a sentence — an
article you want to keep, trip notes from a friend, a training plan
from a forum — press `⌘⇧V` (**Paste & route**), pick the thread,
paste, done. Claude classifies it into the right spot (a doc, the
runbook, your field notes, or just the bench) and tells you what it
did. There's an **Undo** on the thread page if it guessed wrong.
Forwarding emails straight in is coming (§14); until then, paste the
body.

**An honest note about phones.** Mobile capture is on the roadmap but
not built yet (§14). Until it lands, Steep capture happens at the
desk — keep your phone's notes app as the field-capture relay and
empty it onto the bench when you sit down. Imperfect, workable,
temporary.

## 4. Sort — two minutes of deciding, in bulk

Once a day-ish, open **The bench** (nav rail). Sort is keyboard-first:
`J`/`K` walks the list, and each item gets exactly one decision:

| Key | Decision | In life terms |
|---|---|---|
| `A` | Pick up | "I'm doing this soon" — it joins **In focus** |
| `P` | Set aside | "Interesting, not now" — onto the Reflecting shelf |
| `S` | Attach | "This serves that" — link it to a goal, idea, or thread |
| `C` | Crystallize | "That's not a task, that's a *keeper*" |
| `V` | Convert | Wrong kind — flip idea↔note↔action↔question, or spawn a doc/reference |
| `D` | Done | Already handled it |
| `X` | Let go | Noise. Gone. No ceremony. |

The Thursday bench above, sorted in about ninety seconds:

> audiobooks idea → `P` (shelf — fun to think about, no urgency)
> Le Guin line → `C`, type **quote** (a keeper, not a to-do)
> passport → `S` → attach to the *japan-2027* thread → `A` (do this week)
> knee question → `V` already a *question* → `P` (watch it for two more runs)
> sister-POV idea → `S` → attach to the novel's "restructure act one" idea
> dishwasher → `A`
> pacing calculator → `V` → *reference* (it's a link, not a thought)
> gift idea → tag it `#gift-ideas` in the preview pane, then `P`

Notice what you did *not* do: schedule anything, prioritize anything,
or feel anything. Eight decisions, each costing one keystroke and
none of them final — everything is recoverable from search forever.

**Attach is the secret weapon for goal-shaped lives.** `S` links an
action to the thing it serves. Crystallize your actual goal once —
*"Run Chicago 2027 under 4:00"* — and attach training actions to it
as they come. The dashboard then shows your scattered to-dos
*organized under your goals*, which is the view your brain can't
build on its own at 7 a.m.

## 5. The Reflecting shelf — where "not now" lives honestly

Half a curious life is "not now." The **Reflecting** lens (nav rail)
is where all your `P` decisions wait — listed longest-shelved first,
with no dates and no nagging.

Visit it when you're in browsing mood — Sunday coffee is the classic.
Walk it with `J`/`K`:

- `U` — pick it back up (the audiobook idea, now that the 90-minute
  runs have started)
- `C` — it quietly became true while shelved; crystallize it
- `F` — file it (kept, searchable, out of sight)
- `X` — let it go (the enthusiasm was the whole experience, and
  that's fine)

The reflecting count on each thread card links here pre-filtered to
that thread — useful when you're *in* novel-mood and want to see only
the novel's shelved ideas.

**Reframe:** this shelf is not a backlog. It's proof that you can
have an idea, honor it, and *still not owe it anything.*

## 6. Aging — amnesty for everything you dropped

You will drop things. The system assumes it. Anything untouched past
your threshold (Settings → Lifecycle; default 21 days) appears on the
**Aging board**, oldest first — and the whole screen is designed
around one truth: *most cold things should be let go of, guilt-free.*

`J`/`K` to walk, then per item: `U` pick it back up (rare), `C`
crystallize the lesson hiding in it (occasional), `F` file it (the
honest default). Each decision is one keystroke; a 20-item sweep
takes three minutes.

**Make it a ritual, not a reaction.** First Sunday of the month:
amnesty day. Everything cold gets a one-keystroke verdict. You'll
file 80% of it and feel lighter, not worse — because the screen
frames it as *sweeping the workshop*, not *confronting your
failures.*

## 7. Crystals — the part that's actually about your life

Here's the shift that makes Steep different from a to-do app: tasks
are the *least* important thing in it. The point is what settles out
of all the doing and reading and running and thinking — the
**crystals**. Five types, and they map to a life uncannily well:

| Type | What it holds | Real examples |
|---|---|---|
| **principle** | A truth you learned and want to keep operating by | *"I don't get injured from running too much — I get injured from increasing too fast."* |
| **quote** | Someone else's words that did something to you | *"The end justifies the means only if the end is real." — Le Guin* |
| **reminder** | Advice to your future self | *"Book flights on the 11-week mark, not the 2-week panic."* |
| **hint** | A small trick that works | *"Drafting goes better at the library — no kitchen to clean."* |
| **memory** | A moment you want to keep | *"Fushimi Inari at 6 a.m., before the crowds — the fox statues in fog."* |

Make one with `C` anywhere (Sort, Aging, Reflecting), or promote a
doc from its header. On the crystal's page, set the type and — if you
like — attach the material behind it: the *Built on* panel links it
to the field notes, guidebook, runbook, or docs it grew from, and the
**Attach** picker makes that two clicks.

**Example — a book becomes a crystal.** You finish *The Dispossessed*.
The doc where you kept notes gets promoted: **Promote to
crystallization** → type *principle* → title: *"Walls are made by the
people on both sides."* On its page you attach the reading thread's
field-notes section *What I know*, and the quote-crystal from earlier
shows up under **Distilled into** on the original capture. The book
is now load-bearing in your system, not just "read."

### Resurfacing — the system that remembers FOR you

Every crystal carries a quiet timer (default 30 days). When it
lapses, the crystal appears in the **Worth revisiting** slot on your
dashboard — three at a time, with a "show all" toggle if more are
due. Three buttons:

- **Still true** — yes, that's still how I run/write/travel. Timer
  resets; the crystal page proudly counts *"re-surfaced 5× · kept
  fresh."*
- **Revisit** — open it and rework it; your principles are allowed
  to evolve.
- **Retire** — it served its season. Filed, searchable, no longer
  circulating.

This is the loop that makes capture worth it: in October, sweating
the marathon taper, the dashboard hands you the March injury
principle *exactly when you'd otherwise relearn it the hard way.*

## 8. Thread tours — four lives, four shapes

The same furniture (field notes, runbook, guidebook, docs, trace) is
in every thread. Different lives use different rooms.

### The reading thread

- **Guidebook = your reading pipeline.** An ordered spine of entries
  — each book a reference (or a notes doc once you start it), with
  your one-line *why this made the list*. Drag to reorder as
  enthusiasm shifts. Pin it to the thread landing.
- **Captures while reading** → bench → mostly `C` as *quote*, or
  `P` for "ideas this book gave me."
- **Field notes → What I know**: the running synthesis. What is all
  this reading adding up to?
- The thread's crystal gallery becomes, over a year, *a museum of
  what books did to you.* That's the artifact. Visit it.

### The marathon thread

- **Runbook = race-day playbook.** The six sections fit perfectly:
  *Prerequisites* (qualifying, registration windows), *Setup* (gear
  list, fueling plan), *Run* (weekly structure), *Deploy* — race day
  itself: wake time, kit layout, pacing bands, *Troubleshoot* (the
  knee protocol, wall strategies), *Notes*. Every save bumps a
  revision, so the playbook visibly matures between races.
- **The goal is a crystal**; training actions attach to it with `S`,
  so the dashboard shows your runs and errands grouped under the
  race, not loose.
- **The knee question** lives as a `question` item — visible in the
  field notes' *Open questions* (flip the section to managed mode and
  it lists your live question items automatically). When you finally
  learn the answer: `C` → *principle*.

### The trip thread

- **Research phase:** Paste & route everything — the friend's Kyoto
  email, the blog post, the booking confirmations. A *Where to eat*
  guidebook collects references with your one-line notes ("the tiny
  tempura counter — book exactly 30 days out").
- **Action phase:** passport, flights, ryokan — actions attached to
  the thread, picked up as their windows open. The *reminder* crystal
  from last trip ("book at 11 weeks, not the 2-week panic")
  resurfaces on cue, because past-you set it up.
- **After:** this is the part nobody's system does — crystallize the
  **memories**. Three or four *memory* crystals, then file the
  leftover items and let the thread go quiet. The trip stays; the
  logistics evaporate. Years later, *Fushimi Inari in fog* still
  resurfaces some random Tuesday, and that's the whole point.

### The novel thread

- **Captures are sparks**: lines, scene fragments, the sister-POV
  idea. Most go to the shelf; drafting mood is when you walk the
  shelf with `U`.
- **Docs are the workbench** — a doc per chapter or per problem,
  markdown with live preview, revisions counted, comments in the
  margin rail.
- **Trace is your antidote to "I've lost the plot of my own
  project."** *"Go back through your thinking"* on the thread landing
  shows the whole evolution newest-first — crystals as milestones,
  the abandoned epistolary experiment struck through but *visible*.
  Dead ends displayed as part of the work, not erased: unreasonably
  comforting after a three-week gap.

## 9. Tags — threads hold projects; tags hold *themes*

Some things cut across every thread: gift ideas live in reading
(*the seed-saving book*), in the trip (*that knife shop in Kyoto*),
in random captures. That's what shared tags are for.

Tag an item in its edit modal or the Sort preview pane; tag a doc in
its editor rail. Names are lowercase-with-dashes (`gift-ideas`,
`2027`, `with-sam`). Then:

- **Search** `tag:#gift-ideas` → every gift thought you've ever had,
  across all threads, including filed ones. Run it the week before a
  birthday. This single query justifies the habit.
- **Sort** shows tag chips with counts when your bench items carry
  tags — click to filter.

Keep the vocabulary tiny — three to six tags you'll actually
remember. Tags you have to *recall* are tags you won't use.

## 10. The weekly review — fifteen minutes that hold it together

If capture is the habit that feeds the system, the **Weekly review**
(Dashboard button) is the habit that makes it trustworthy. It's
read-only on purpose: counts for the week (captured, picked up,
crystallized, set aside, let go), the crystals that landed, the
activity log, and what's going cold — every row clickable.

The ritual, fifteen minutes, same time weekly (attach it to something
that already happens — Sunday coffee):

1. Read the shape: *34 captured, 2 crystallized, 11 let go.* A heavy
   capture week with little sorting? Fine — now you know.
2. Click into anything that makes you go "wait, what was that?"
3. Jump to **Reflecting** for a shelf walk if the mood is right.
4. Glance at the aging section; if it's swelling, book amnesty day.

What the review is *not*: a performance evaluation. Eleven let-gos is
not failure, it's the system digesting. The screen literally tells
you: recognize the shape of the week, don't act on every line.

## 11. Claude as your body double

Everything in §10 of the main guide applies, but for life-management
the connected-Claude moves are different in flavor. Once your Claude
client is connected to Steep's MCP server (one config block — see the
main guide §11), you can talk *about* your system instead of
operating it:

- *"What's on my bench? Sort the obvious noise out and tell me what
  actually needs me."* — body-doubling through triage on a
  low-executive-function day.
- *"Read my marathon field notes and the runbook, and draft a taper
  plan for the last three weeks into the Run section."*
- *"What's gone cold in the novel thread? Summarize where my thinking
  left off."* — re-entry after a long gap, without archaeology.
- *"Capture these as ideas in the trip thread: …"* — brain-dump a
  whole commute's worth of thoughts in one message.

The **Ask Claude** button on every thread, doc, and notebook copies a
correctly-targeted prompt to your clipboard — paste it into Claude
and go. (A fully in-app chat is on the roadmap, §14.)

## 12. A week in this life

**Sunday, 9:00.** Coffee. Weekly review: 31 captured, 1 crystal, 8
let go. Reflecting shelf walk: pick the audiobooks idea back up (`U`),
let two stale novel sparks go (`X`, `X`). *Worth revisiting* offers
the flight-booking reminder — **Still true**, and oh, it's 12 weeks
out: capture `book Kyoto flights` → Sort → `S` attach to japan-2027 →
`A`.

**Monday–Friday, scattered.** Eleven captures: four novel sparks, two
knee observations, a recipe, the seed-saving book (tagged
`#gift-ideas`), three errands. Zero filing decisions at capture time.
Wednesday's bench sort: ninety seconds, mostly `P` and `A`, one `X`.

**Tuesday, 19:00.** Long run done. The knee held — that's two good
runs in the new shoes. Open the question item, `C` → *principle*:
*"The knee thing was a shoe problem."* Attach the runbook
(Troubleshoot section already had the protocol — two clicks in the
Built on panel).

**Saturday, 10:00.** Drafting mood, rare and precious. Novel thread →
Reflecting filtered to it → `U` on the sister-POV idea → two hours in
the chapter-3 doc. Before closing: one capture — `the sister knows
about the letters all along` — because the next idea always arrives
while executing the last one, and now it has somewhere to go.

Nothing pinged you. Nothing went red. The marathon advanced, the trip
got cheaper, the novel grew, and forty stray thoughts ended the week
either *placed* or *released* instead of orbiting your head at 2 a.m.

## 13. Troubleshooting for human brains

**"I stopped opening it for two weeks."** Nothing broke. Nothing is
red. Open Sort, sweep the bench (most of it will be `X` — old
thoughts expire honestly), hit the Aging board for amnesty, and
you're current in ten minutes. The system is designed to be abandoned
and re-entered; re-entry cost is deliberately near zero.

**"My bench has 60 items and I'm avoiding it."** Don't sort it
properly. Speed-run it: anything that doesn't spark recognition in
one second gets `X`. You are allowed to let go of 50 of them — search
keeps everything findable if one turns out to matter. A clear bench
you half-trusted beats a full bench you avoid.

**"I made 14 threads in my first week."** Classic, and fine. Unpin
the ones that aren't alive; fold strays into `ideas`. Threads are
for *ongoing* lines of life — a single errand never needed its own
thread, and nothing is lost by consolidating.

**"I capture but never crystallize."** Watch for the tell at Sort: an
item you're about to file that makes you think *"well, the lesson
was…"* — that sentence is a crystal. `C`, pick the type, move on.
One a week is a great rate; this is a museum, not a quota.

**"Resurfacing shows me stuff I don't care about anymore."**
**Retire** is the right answer, not a sad one. A crystal that stopped
being true was true for a season — filing it is the system working.

**"I want it on my phone."** So does everyone — see §14. Until then:
phone notes app as the relay, emptied onto the bench at the desk.
